Rutherford scattering Relevant

Mar 2026With a lab partnerExcel · LaTeX
Read the report (PDF)

Does alpha scattering off a thin metal foil fall off as 1/sin⁴(φ/2), the signature of a small, dense nucleus?

What I did

Ran the scattering chamber under vacuum with a sealed alpha source, a collimated beam and a detector on a rotating arm. Counted alpha particles for about 60 seconds at each angle from 5° to 30°, first with gold foil and then aluminum, repeated the measurement at each angle, and took background counts under the same conditions. Followed radiation-safety rules throughout: minimal handling, keeping distance, and the source shielded when not in use.

Analysis

Converted counts to rates and linearized the model to ln R = −4 ln sin(φ/2) + C, so theory predicts a slope of −4. Propagated Poisson counting uncertainty (σ = √N) into ln R and fit each target.

Result

Gold gave a slope of −4.52, within 13% of theory, with the steep fall-off a point-like nucleus predicts.

What limited it

Aluminum produced only a handful of counts past 15°, so counting noise dominated and the log transform amplified it. Background, the detector’s finite width and possible multiple scattering in the foil add systematic error at large angles.

Gold and aluminum foil
Angles

Millikan oil drop Relevant

Jan 2026With three lab partnersExcel · LaTeX
Read the report (PDF)

Is electric charge quantized, and how big is the smallest unit?

What I did

Sprayed oil droplets between two plates 6 mm apart and followed single droplets through a USB microscope camera. Calibrated the camera with a ruler in the focal plane, then timed each droplet falling with no field and rising at 500 V, at least ten times each, for five positive and five negative droplets with the polarity reversed between sets. Logged room temperature and pressure for the viscosity and slip corrections, and switched the high voltage off before putting anything in the optical path.

Analysis

Stokes’ law turned fall velocity into droplet radius, and the force balance under the field gave each droplet’s charge, with a Cunningham slip correction for droplets close to the mean free path of air. Timing uncertainty, as the standard error of the mean, was propagated through radius and charge.

What limited it

Brownian motion made the timings jitter and was the largest source of random error. Droplets that drifted out of focus were dropped, because drift changes the distance calibration.

Cavendish torsion balance Relevant

Mar 2026With a lab partnerExcel · LaTeX
Read the report (PDF)

Can the gravitational pull between lab-sized masses be measured with a torsion balance, and what value of G does it give?

What I did

Let the balance settle for about 45 minutes, then tracked a laser beam reflected off the balance mirror onto a linear photodiode array 0.81 m away, logging the spot position over time. Ran it twice, moving the large masses between runs.

Analysis

Picked six successive peaks to get the oscillation period, fit ln(amplitude) against time for the damping rate, converted the 23.5-pixel spot shift into an angle with the optical-lever relation, then derived the fibre’s torsion constant and G.

Result

G = (9.77 ± 0.18) × 10⁻¹¹ m³ kg⁻¹ s⁻², about 46% above the accepted 6.674 × 10⁻¹¹.

What limited it

The report traces the gap to geometry and environment: G depends on the square of the mass separation, the lab wasn’t vibration-isolated, and the second run had no settling time. Proposed fixes: vibration isolation, tighter alignment and less manual peak-picking.
